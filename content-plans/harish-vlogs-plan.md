# 2. Channel Positioning

## Recommended channel positioning

**Harish Vlogs** follows the real-life journey of a German Shepherd growing up in a family home, including daily routines, beginner training, puppy care, family relationships, progress, mistakes, and products the family genuinely uses.

## Audience promise

> “Every Harish Vlogs episode gives German Shepherd owners and future puppy parents one honest, useful, or emotionally relatable moment from real family life—with practical lessons they can adapt to their own dog.”

## Primary audience

1. **Current German Shepherd puppy owners**
   - Looking for routines, training ideas, enrichment, grooming, and realistic expectations.

2. **People considering a German Shepherd**
   - Want to understand the time, patience, cost, exercise, and family commitment involved.

3. **Family and pet-story viewers**
   - Enjoy emotional progress, funny moments, bonding, and daily life.

4. **Dog-product shoppers**
   - Interested in honest demonstrations of useful, age-appropriate products.

## Recommended content pillars

| Pillar | Approximate share | Content examples |
|---|---:|---|
| German Shepherd puppy care and daily life | 35% | First month, food routine, sleep, grooming, safe enrichment |
| Humane beginner training | 30% | Name recognition, recall foundations, calm greetings, leash introduction |
| Real family stories | 20% | Family rules, challenges, funny moments, progress, emotional milestones |
| Relevant products and home setup | 10% | Beds, leads, toys, grooming tools, storage, puppy-proofing |
| Community and viewer questions | 5% | Q&A, polls, responses to common puppy-owner problems |

These percentages are a planning guide, not a rigid requirement.

## Recurring series

### Series 1: Raising Our German Shepherd

Long-form episodes documenting real progress over time.

### Series 2: German Shepherd Training at Home

Short, repeatable training demonstrations using calm, reward-based methods.

### Series 3: GSD Family Life

Stories about how the dog fits into the household, family routines, celebrations, challenges, and everyday moments.

### Series 4: Gear We Actually Use

Honest reviews and demonstrations of products that are relevant to German Shepherd care. Sponsored or affiliate relationships should always be disclosed.

## Editorial rules

- Use real footage and real outcomes.
- Do not promise that one method will work for every dog.
- Describe health and nutrition choices as personal experience or veterinarian-directed decisions.
- Avoid showing unsafe off-leash situations, dangerous feeding, or forced interactions.
- Use reward-based, low-stress training.
- Do not stage fear, injury, distress, or dangerous behavior for views.
- Disclose sponsorships, gifted products, and affiliate links.
- Do not buy views, subscribers, likes, comments, or watch time.
- Avoid “guaranteed result” language such as “train any German Shepherd in one day.”
- Obtain family consent before filming and avoid showing private addresses, documents, or sensitive information.

# 3. Packaging and Viewer-Conversion Strategy

## Long-form structure

Each 10–14 minute video should follow this pattern:

1. **0:00–0:20 — Cold open**
   - Show the most useful, funny, surprising, or emotional moment first.
2. **0:20–0:45 — Clear promise**
   - Explain what viewers will learn or experience.
3. **0:45–2:00 — Context**
   - Explain the dog’s age, situation, and the family’s goal.
4. **2:00–8:30 — Main story or demonstration**
   - Include real attempts, pauses, mistakes, and outcomes.
5. **8:30–10:30 — Honest reflection**
   - Explain what worked, what did not, and what would be changed.
6. **10:30–12:00 — Practical recap**
   - Give a simple checklist.
7. **Final 20 seconds — CTA**
   - Send the viewer to the next related video or playlist.

## Thumbnail rules

- Use one clear dog expression or family moment.
- Keep text to two or three words.
- Use high contrast.
- Avoid cluttered collages.
- Use the same visual style across the month.
- Test emotional curiosity against practical clarity.

Suggested thumbnail text:

- **30 DAYS LATER**
- **7 TRAINING CUES**
- **A REAL DAY**
- **WHAT CHANGED?**
- **FIRST WEEK**
- **OUR BIGGEST MISTAKE**

**Shot list**

**Dialogue**

#### 3:30–4:40 — Cue 3: Touch

**Shot list**

1. Present open palm.

3. Reward nose-to-hand contact.
4. Move hand slightly farther away.
5. Repeat.

**Dialogue**

> “Touch is useful because it gives the puppy a simple target and helps us move the dog without pulling. We present an open hand, wait for the nose to come toward it, and reward contact.”
>
> “We keep the hand still. If we move it too quickly, the exercise becomes a chase rather than a calm target.”

#### 4:40–6:00 — Cue 4: Come

**Shot list**

1. Secure indoor or enclosed space.

3. Say “come” in a cheerful voice.

**Dialogue**

> “For recall, safety comes first. We practice in a secure area and never test an unreliable recall near roads, traffic, or other hazards.”
>
> “We say the cue once, make ourselves inviting, and reward the puppy for coming toward us. We avoid calling the puppy for something unpleasant during early practice, because we want the cue to remain positive.”
>
> “This is a foundation exercise. It is not a guarantee that the puppy will come in every situation.”

#### 6:00–7:10 — Cue 5: Wait

**Shot list**

1. Puppy sits or stands calmly.
2. Show a one-second pause.
3. Reward.
4. Gradually increase the pause.
5. Demonstrate a reset after the puppy moves.

**Dialogue**

> “For wait, we begin with a very small pause. One second can be enough at first. We reward the pause, then release the puppy.”
>
> “If the puppy moves, we do not scold. We simply reduce the time and try again. The lesson is that waiting is temporary and predictable.”

#### 7:10–8:25 — Cue 6: Leave it

**Shot list**

1. Place a safe, low-value object in a closed hand.
2. Puppy investigates.
3. Wait for the puppy to look away.
4. Reward with a separate reward.
5. Progress to an object on the floor with supervision.

**Dialogue**

> “We start with a safe object and a closed hand. The instant the puppy disengages, we reward from the other hand.”
>
> “We do not put unsafe items in front of the puppy for entertainment. We also supervise this exercise closely and use a professional trainer if the puppy is guarding objects or showing concerning behavior.”

#### 8:25–9:35 — Cue 7: Settle

**Shot list**

1. Quiet mat or bed.
2. Reward calm body language.
3. Sit nearby without exciting the puppy.
4. Offer a calm toy if appropriate.

**Dialogue**

> “Settle is less about a perfect position and more about rewarding calm behavior. We place the mat in a quiet area, wait for the puppy to lower the energy, and reward that calm moment.”
>
> “We are not forcing the puppy to stay still. We are showing that quiet behavior is also valuable.”

- Using too many repetitions.

**Voiceover**

- [ ] Capture at least one imperfect attempt or reset.
- [ ] Capture establishing shots of the room and routine.
- [ ] Record clear spoken explanations without background noise.
- [ ] Film the dog only when comfortable; do not interrupt needed rest.
- [ ] Keep training sessions short and stop if the puppy becomes distressed.
- [ ] Record an explicit CTA and next-video recommendation.
- [ ] Capture a thumbnail frame with a clear facial expression or action.

## C. Long-form editing checklist

- [ ] Place the strongest moment before the channel intro.
- [ ] Remove long greetings, repeated explanations, and dead air.
- [ ] Add chapter cards or visual transitions every 30–90 seconds where helpful.
- [ ] Add readable subtitles to important spoken points.
- [ ] Use date, age, and progress labels only when accurate.
- [ ] Keep safety or professional-guidance notes near the relevant claim.
- [ ] Show both successes and realistic limitations.
- [ ] Confirm all music and footage are licensed or original.
- [ ] Add description, keywords, hashtags, chapters, and playlist link.
- [ ] Add an end screen to the next most relevant episode.
- [ ] Add a pinned comment with a genuine question and next-video link.
- [ ] Watch the final export on a phone before scheduling.

## D. Shorts production checklist

- [ ] Put movement or the main visual in the first frame.
- [ ] State the hook within the first two seconds.
- [ ] Use one idea per Short.
- [ ] Keep the subject large enough to read on a phone.
- [ ] Add burned-in captions or on-screen text.
- [ ] Avoid text that covers the dog’s face or important action.
- [ ] Remove pauses, repeated attempts, and unnecessary setup.
- [ ] End immediately after the payoff.
- [ ] Use a relevant SEO title without exaggerating the outcome.
- [ ] Add a concise description and four or five relevant hashtags.
- [ ] Link to the related long-form video or playlist.
- [ ] Disclose any sponsorship or affiliate relationship.

## E. Responsible dog-content checklist

- [ ] Do not stage fear, injury, panic, or unsafe behavior.
- [ ] Do not use dangerous objects or food for a training demonstration.
- [ ] Do not encourage children to handle a dog unsupervised.
- [ ] Do not show unreliable recall near roads, traffic, or hazards.
- [ ] Use secure areas for recall and leash practice.
- [ ] Stop filming if the puppy shows distress, pain, fear, or exhaustion.
- [ ] Remove damaged toys or unsafe equipment.
- [ ] Use age-appropriate and professional guidance for exercise, food, vaccinations, and health.
- [ ] Present training as an individual experience rather than a guaranteed result.
- [ ] Keep the dog’s comfort more important than obtaining a shot.

# 11. Success Definition for the First 30 Days

The first month should not be judged only by whether a single video goes viral.

A successful month would show:

- More viewers watching multiple German Shepherd videos.
- An increase from the 2.3% returning-viewer baseline.
- Better subscriber conversion per 1,000 views.
- At least one long-form topic that earns sustained watch time.
- Shorts sending viewers to related long-form videos.
- A growing set of genuine viewer questions.
- Clear evidence about whether training, care, family stories, or product demonstrations produce the strongest returning audience.

The central objective is to convert the channel’s existing reach into a recognizable, trustworthy German Shepherd series that viewers choose to follow month after month.
