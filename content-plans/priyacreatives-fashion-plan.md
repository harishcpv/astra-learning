## What this means

### 1. Shorts are the discovery engine

Shorts are successfully bringing new people into the channel. Continue using them, but every Short should point toward a related long-form video, playlist, or next episode.

### 2. Long-form is the deeper relationship engine

Although long-form receives fewer views, it produces a much larger share of watch time. This suggests that viewers who choose a longer video are more engaged.

The next growth opportunity is to create a clear path:

> Short discovery -> related 10+ minute tutorial -> playlist -> subscription

### 3. The CTR is a good foundation

A 6.2% CTR suggests the channel packaging is working reasonably well. The goal is not to change everything, but to improve clarity:

- Show the finished result in every thumbnail.
- Use 2–4 words of thumbnail text.
- Put the main search phrase near the beginning of the title.
- Make the first 15 seconds visually match the promise of the title and thumbnail.

**Visuals**

**Spoken script**

> “For this tutorial I’m using white powder for the outline and three main colors for the filling. You can use any colors you already have. If you are making this for a festival, choose one bright color, one warm color, and white. That combination usually gives a very clean result.
>
> Before starting, wipe the floor and let it dry completely. A slightly dusty surface makes the powder spread unevenly. I’m also keeping a small cloth nearby because cleaning as we work is much easier than trying to clean everything at the end.

#### 01:45–04:00 — Design 1: Simple flower rangoli

**Visuals**

- Mark the center
- Place an even dot grid

- Fill petals with two colors
- Add small white accents

**Spoken script**

> “For the first design, I’m starting with a small dot grid. I’m using five dots in the center row and reducing the dots on each side. The exact number is not important. What matters is keeping the left and right sides similar.
>
> Now I’m connecting the center dots into soft petals. I’m not pressing too hard because a light line is easier to correct. Once the first flower is complete, I’ll add four smaller petals around it. This gives us a flower shape without needing a complicated pattern.
>
> I’m filling the larger petals with pink and using yellow only in the center. Using one color for the main shape and a second color for the center keeps the design from looking crowded.
>
> If one line becomes too thick, do not wipe the whole rangoli. Use a dry finger or a small piece of paper to remove only the extra powder. Small corrections are normal, especially when you are learning.”

Film the dot placement from overhead. Pause the voiceover while drawing two or three petals, then resume with a close-up explanation. This creates a natural 2-minute demonstration segment.

#### 04:00–06:20 — Design 2: Diya-inspired rangoli

**Visuals**

- Draw a curved diya shape
- Add flame motifs
- Add dots around the border
- Use orange, yellow, and white
- Add symmetrical side details

**Spoken script**

> “For the second design, I’m creating a diya-inspired shape. First, I’m drawing a curved bowl in the center. Think of it like a wide smile. I’m keeping the two sides the same height, then adding a small flame above it.
>
> Around the diya, I’m adding small dots. These dots are useful because they fill empty space without making the design difficult. If your hand is not comfortable drawing long curves, create the curve using three short sections. From a little distance, it will still look like one smooth line.
>
> I’m using orange in the bowl, yellow in the flame, and white for the outer details. The warm colors make this design especially suitable for a festival entrance, pooja corner, or the space near a diya arrangement.
>
> At this stage, step back and look at the rangoli from a standing position. When we work very close to the floor, small details can appear larger than they really are.”

Show an imperfect curve briefly and demonstrate the three-section correction. Include a wide shot after the design is complete so viewers understand the final scale.

#### 06:20–09:00 — Design 3: Lotus and border rangoli

**Visuals**

- Create a central lotus
- Add curved leaves
- Add a circular border
- Fill with blue, pink, and white
- Add small repeated dots

**Spoken script**

> “The third design looks more detailed, but it is still made from simple shapes. I’m starting with a small lotus in the center. The first petal is straight, the next petals curve outward, and the side petals are slightly smaller. Repeating the same shape makes the design look intentional.
>
> Around the lotus, I’m adding four leaves. I’m leaving enough space between the flower and the leaves so the colors do not merge when I fill them. This is one of the easiest ways to make a rangoli look neat: leave a little breathing space between each section.
>
> Now I’m creating a circular border with small white dots. You can use a bottle cap to lightly mark the circle, or draw it freehand. The border does not have to be perfect. The repeated dots will make it look balanced.
>
> I’m using blue for the leaves, pink for the lotus, and white to separate the shapes. If you want a traditional look, you can replace blue with green. If you want a softer look, use pastel pink and yellow.”

Use a time-lapse for repetitive dot placement, but keep the outline and color-filling steps in real time. Cut between overhead, hand close-up, and standing wide shot to maintain visual variety.

#### 09:00–10:30 — Filling techniques and common mistakes

**Visuals**

- Close-up of powder between two fingers
- Demonstrate thin and thick lines
- Correct an uneven petal
- Clean excess powder
- Compare a crowded section with a balanced section

**Spoken script**

> “Before the final reveal, here are three quick corrections that make a big difference. First, pick up a small amount of powder between two fingers instead of holding a large amount in your hand. This gives you more control.
>
> Second, fill from the center of each shape toward the edge. If you start at the outside, it is easier to cross the outline. Third, if two colors mix, do not panic. Let the powder settle, remove the extra color with a folded paper edge, and redraw the white outline.
>
> The goal is not mathematical perfection. A rangoli is handmade, so small differences give it character. The main things to look for are clean color blocks, enough empty space, and a balanced overall shape.”

Use captions for each correction: “small amount,” “center outward,” and “redraw the border.”

# 9. Repeatable Video Formula

Use this structure for future videos:

### First 5 seconds

Show the finished result.

### First 15 seconds

Explain exactly what the viewer will learn or make.

### Minute 1

Show materials, outfit pieces, or the color palette.

### Middle section

Teach the process in visible stages. Change the camera angle or visual every 5–10 seconds where possible.

### Final minute

Show the finished result from multiple angles.

### Final CTA

Ask one specific question and direct the viewer to one related video.

Recommended CTA format:

> “If you want to make the matching decoration, watch the next video in this playlist. Comment with the design or look you want me to create next, and subscribe to Priyacreatives & Fashion.”
